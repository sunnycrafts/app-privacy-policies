Privacy Policy for Wordprint Vocabulary
Effective: 12 November 2026. Applies to version 1.0.0 and later versions until a new policy is shown.
App: Wordprint Vocabulary (Android package com.sunny.wordprint.vocabulary)
Developer: SunnyCrafts. Contact: developer.sunnycrafts@gmail.com

1. The short version
Wordprint Vocabulary does not collect, store on any server, share or sell your personal data. There is no account, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking. The app's own code never connects to the internet and sends nothing off your phone. (Android lists an internet permission for the app only because the Google Play Billing component, used for the purchase, declares it.) The only information the developer can ever receive is anonymous crash and freeze reports (section 5), and only through Google, and only from phones whose owners chose to share diagnostics.

2. What stays on your phone
Your progress (word cards, streaks, scores, typing and writing history, mistakes, favorites and word lists), your settings (including the name, picture or emoji, colours and voice you choose to personalize the app) and a small activity log are saved in the app's private storage on your phone. The log records actions, counts and error types, never your answers, writing or recordings. This data is not sent anywhere and is not included in Android cloud backups or device transfer. It is deleted when you uninstall the app or use Reset my progress in Settings. If you pick a picture, it is copied in a small size into the app's private storage; the app never reads your other photos.

Daily reminder (optional, off by default). In Me, Settings you can choose a time for one daily reminder. It is a notification made by the app on your phone at the hour you pick; it is skipped on days you have already practised. Nothing about it is sent anywhere. On Android 13 and later the app asks for the notification permission only when you pick a time. If you refuse, or choose Off, no reminder is made and the rest of the app works as before. The app also asks Android to plan the reminder again after the phone restarts.

3. Microphone and speech
The app asks for the microphone permission only when you tap Record in Speaking practice, and records only while the recording is open. The recording is turned into text by Android's on-device speech recognizer, which runs on your phone. The recording file is deleted right after it is scored. The app never stores your voice and never sends it to the developer. Spoken examples use your phone's own text-to-speech voice. Your phone's speech and voice services are governed by their own providers' policies.

4. Free trial and purchase
The app is free to use for 30 days. The start of the trial is read from Android's own record of when the app was installed, or from an earlier trial date saved in a progress file you import yourself (the earlier date is used); nothing is sent to us. After the trial, a one-time purchase (US$4.99, or the local price shown by Google Play) unlocks all practice permanently. The purchase is made through Google Play Billing. Google processes your payment and handles your payment details, name and e-mail under Google's own privacy policy; the developer does not receive them. The app stores only a yes/no flag on your phone saying the purchase was made, and asks Google Play on each start whether your account owns it, so you can restore it on a new phone. Google Play may share purchase order records with the developer through the Play Console for accounting and refunds.

5. Crash and freeze reports (the only data the developer can receive)
When the app crashes or stops responding (an "ANR"), Google's Android vitals can include a technical report in the developer's Google Play Console, if you have chosen to share usage and diagnostics data in your Android settings. A report contains technical details such as the app version, device model, Android version, the time and the error trace. It does not contain your name, e-mail, answers, writing, recordings or any identifier added by this app. The developer uses these reports only to find and fix crashes and freezes. You can stop them at any time by turning off "Usage & diagnostics" in your Android settings.

You can also choose to send feedback or a problem report yourself (Me, Send Feedback). It is a message you can read and edit, with the app version, phone model, Android version and, if you leave the switch on, the app's own log, which never contains your answers, writing or recordings. The app only prepares the message and opens your own e-mail app; nothing is sent unless you press send there. In Me, Settings, Data you can share your own progress as a file or text summary. That is your choice and the app itself never uploads it.

6. Sharing and selling
The developer does not sell your data, does not share it with advertisers or data brokers and does not use it for advertising or profiling. The app contains no third-party advertising or analytics code. The only third-party component that talks to a service is Google Play Billing (section 4).

7. Children
The app is not aimed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal data from anyone, because it collects no personal data at all.

8. Your rights and deleting data
Because the developer holds no personal data about you, there is nothing to access or delete on our side. Everything the app stores is on your phone, and you can erase it at any time with Reset my progress in Settings or by uninstalling the app. For purchase records or crash data held by Google, see Google's privacy controls.

9. Security
The app asks for the microphone permission (only when you use Speaking practice), the notification permission (only if you turn on the optional daily reminder), a permission to plan that reminder again after a restart, and the permissions that Google Play Billing declares (billing, internet and network state, used only for the purchase). The app's own code makes no network connections, keeps its data in private app storage and turns Android app backup off.

10. Changes to this policy
If this policy changes, the new version will be shown in the app (Me, Privacy Policy) and on the store page with a new effective date. If a change ever involved collecting more data, the app would ask for your consent first.
